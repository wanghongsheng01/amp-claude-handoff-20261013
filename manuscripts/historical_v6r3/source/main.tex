% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
The global challenge of microbial infections and antibiotic resistance motivates the design of antimicrobial peptides (AMPs). This paper describes a two-stage model combining a sequence-based variational autoencoder (VAE), physicochemical descriptors, and class-conditioned diffusion. The retrieved implementation models the posterior mean and log-standard-deviation tensors; a latent is sampled after reverse diffusion and decoded by a convolutional decoder. The historical materials document synthesis reports for 27 candidates and a reported six active candidates, but disagree on the activity criterion and denominator. A retrospective count of the supplied MIC summary gives seven candidates with at least one recorded numeric MIC below 128 $\mu$g/mL; undefined entries are retained as unknown. This count does not establish label-specific control, an unconditional generation success rate, or an architectural advantage. The code-to-run linkage, independent evaluation split, selection ledger, and assay aggregation rules require resolution before these claims can be tested.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{ntibiotic} resistance motivates investigation of antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) as candidate antibacterial agents. Reviews describe membrane-disrupting and intracellular mechanisms for different AMPs, together with constraints associated with resistance, host toxicity, and stability~\cite{huan2020antimicrobial,gan2021multifaceted}. These mechanisms do not by themselves establish clinical selectivity, safety, or freedom from resistance for a newly generated sequence.

Computational discovery can identify AMP candidates for testing under specified conditions. For example, Huang et al. screened a library of six- to nine-residue peptides and evaluated selected candidates against bacterial isolates and in mouse pneumonia models~\cite{huang2023identification}. Those results concern the evaluated candidates and study conditions; they do not establish that AMPs as a class provide precise treatment, fewer side effects, or a replacement for broad-spectrum antibiotics. The objective considered here is class-conditioned candidate generation for subsequent experimental assessment.

Experimental AMP development combines sequence design, peptide production, characterization, and biological testing~\cite{huan2020antimicrobial,gan2021multifaceted}. The work needed to produce and assess candidates motivates computational prioritization, including frameworks that combine sequence generation with activity prediction~\cite{li2021pepvae}. Candidate generation, predictor-based screening, and experimental evaluation are distinct steps; success at one step does not establish success at the others.

Deep generative models are studied for molecular design~\cite{meyers2021denovo,zhavoronkov2023generative}, including AMP candidate generation followed by screening or experimental assessment~\cite{das2021accelerated,szymczak2023discovering}. Recurrent neural networks have been applied to molecular generation~\cite{segler2018generating,kotsias2020direct} and peptide sequence design~\cite{muller2018recurrent}. Variational autoencoders (VAEs) learn latent representations using encoder--decoder models~\cite{kingma2014autoencoding}; AMP applications include sequence generation from learned latent spaces~\cite{dean2020variational} and PepVAE, which combines a VAE with antimicrobial activity prediction~\cite{li2021pepvae}. Building on denoising diffusion models~\cite{ho2020denoising}, diffusion-based methods have been reported for protein structure design~\cite{watson2023denovo}, protein sequence generation~\cite{huang2024cpdiffusion}, and AMP generation~\cite{jin2024artificial}. These citations identify method families and applications; their published outcomes are not a matched performance comparison with the present model.

Protein language models provide learned sequence representations~\cite{rives2021biological,elnaggar2021prottrans,brandes2022proteinbert,lin2023evolutionary}, while structure prediction networks address protein or biomolecular structure inference~\cite{jumper2021highly,baek2021accurate,abramson2024accurate}. Graph neural networks have been studied for molecular property prediction~\cite{wieder2020compact,xiong2020pushing,maziarka2024transfer}, and reinforcement learning for molecular design and optimization~\cite{olivecrona2017molecular,zhou2019optimization,wang2023denovo}. These related computational tasks do not by themselves establish antimicrobial activity or the validity of the historical structure predictions examined here.

Two evaluation objectives motivate the present analysis:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The first objective is to assess whether the reported reconstruction and generation results are linked to documented training data, activity labels and independent evaluation partitions. Unlabeled sequence data and sequence-derived descriptors do not supply measured antibacterial outcomes. Their value for generalization requires a controlled comparison with verified partitions.
\item The second objective is to assess whether class conditioning changes measured antibacterial activity after accounting for candidate selection. Related frameworks use activity predictors or additional preselection~\cite{li2021pepvae,szymczak2023discovering}; their effects must be distinguished from those of the generator. A class-conditioned sampling procedure alone does not demonstrate label-specific antibacterial control.
\end{enumerate}

This paper describes a two-stage sequence-and-descriptor model and reconciles its retrieved implementation with the available historical records. The following components define the inspected computational design; their empirical contribution remains to be established:

\textbf{(i) Sequence and descriptor inputs to a VAE:} The retrieved VAE combines residue embeddings with aligned triplet descriptors and a global nine-dimensional descriptor vector. The historical manuscript describes pretraining on UniProt-derived sequences. The actual data snapshot, preprocessing and independent split have not been recovered, so this representation cannot yet be credited with improved robustness or generalization.

\textbf{(ii) Class-conditioned diffusion of posterior parameters:} The retrieved implementation applies diffusion to the VAE posterior mean and log-standard-deviation tensors. After reverse diffusion, a latent is sampled and decoded into a candidate sequence. The sampler uses supplied class labels without querying an activity predictor, but the historical selection procedure uses AMP Scanner. The end-to-end pipeline is therefore not classifier-independent, and label-specific antibacterial control remains an empirical question.

\subsection{Multimodal Deep Generative Probabilistic Model}

To model peptides and specifically generate desired AMPs, a multimodal probabilistic model comprising two stages is proposed, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}. The first stage employs a sequence-based VAE to fit and learn the broad probabilistic distribution and contextual relationships of peptides. The second utilizes a conditionally controlled diffusion generative model; the model focuses on fitting the probability distribution of AMPs with specific functions based on the general probabilistic distribution of known peptides, with the aim of condition-dependent generation. This describes the intended two-stage design; its antibacterial control and advantage over matched alternatives remain to be demonstrated.

\subsubsection{Multimodal Flow-Guided Variational Autoencoder}

As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}, the retrieved VAE accepts three inputs: an amino-acid sequence, one triplet of physicochemical descriptors per residue, and a nine-dimensional global descriptor vector. The implementation determines how these inputs are combined; it does not establish a measured benefit from adding descriptors.

The sequence and triplet inputs share residue positions. The encoder combines their representations using gated convolutions and retains a latent value at each padded sequence position. The retrieved reconstruction objective includes padding positions. These are implementation properties, not evidence of a unique architecture or improved generalization.

The historical ablation values and reconstruction illustrations have no recovered run logs, normalization settings or independent split membership. They are retained in the provenance archive and excluded from the empirical results here. In the retrieved implementation, repeating the global descriptor across positions gives identical attention keys and values; this is equivalent to adding the same projected global vector at each position.

A higher reconstruction loss alone does not demonstrate greater novelty, useful clustering, or better antimicrobial activity. Those hypotheses require same-data, same-budget ablations and outcome-specific measurements.

The retrieved VAE includes a conditional additive coupling flow in its prior/KL term. The diffusion stage models posterior parameters, and final sequence generation samples in posterior space and calls the decoder without an inverse-flow step. The empirical benefit of the flow remains unverified.

The historical report of 98 equal-length reconstructions among 100 examples is preserved in the provenance archive. It cannot establish independent reconstruction performance without the example identities and run/split linkage.

\subsubsection{Residual Characterization with Triplet Descriptor}

The dataset-side script uses RDKit~\cite{landrum2013rdkit} to construct a molecular object directly from each residue. Figure~\ref{fig:triplet_descriptors} identifies the called function. Its active path calculates the following triplet without a SMILES serialization step:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Molecular Weight (MolWt)}: Returns the molecular weight of the constructed molecule. This revision does not recompute the historical per-residue descriptor values.
\item \textbf{Partition Coefficient (LogP)}: Indicates the hydrophobicity and hydrophilicity of the molecule, describing its distribution tendency in polar versus nonpolar environments. Values range from highly hydrophilic (negative LogP) to highly hydrophobic (positive LogP).
\item \textbf{Topological Polar Surface Area (TPSA)}: Describes the surface area of polar atoms in the molecule, typically related to solubility and membrane transport ability. Higher TPSA values indicate greater polarity.
\end{itemize}

For each residue, the dataset-side script constructs a triplet containing MolWt, LogP and TPSA. The resulting triplet sequence follows the original residue sequence. This operation preserves residue order. The separate task-side script uses MolWt, hydrogen-bond acceptor count, and hydrogen-bond donor count instead. No recovered run record identifies which descriptor source produced the historical training inputs.

\subsubsection{Generation of Polypeptide Nonuplet Descriptor}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Total Ring Count}: Number of ring structures in the peptide
\item \textbf{Aromatic Ring Count}: Number of aromatic rings
\item \textbf{Saturated Ring Count}: Number of saturated rings
\item \textbf{Fraction of sp$^3$ Hybridized Carbons (Fsp3)}: Proportion of carbon atoms with sp$^3$ hybridization

\item \textbf{Total Polar Surface Area (TPSA)}: Sum of polar surface areas across the entire molecule

The historical manuscript describes normalized and standardized global descriptors. Their preprocessing implementation and fitted parameters have not been recovered, so that transformation is unverified. \subsection{Multimodal Flow-Guided VAE}

\textbf{Implementation provenance.} The following description aligns the historical manuscript with the retrieved implementation (commit \texttt{e7486fb}). Its linkage to the historical training runs and reported results remains unverified. The description and algorithms specify this source implementation; they do not establish that the reported experiments were reproduced.

\subsubsection{Multimodal Embedding for Encoder}

The VAE input consists of the amino acid sequence $X$, the aligned triplet descriptor sequence $D$, and the global nonuplet $G$. As described in the historical manuscript, a WaveNet-style structure combines residue embeddings and convolutionally projected triplet descriptors through gated convolutional layers and residual connections. The implementation takes the absolute value of the global descriptor before fusion. Its attention module repeats the same global vector at every sequence position; the repeated keys and values make the attention output equivalent to adding the same learned value projection at each position. This implementation therefore does not provide position-specific cross-modal attention weights with distinct value content.

The encoder returns matrices $\mu$ and $\ell=\log\sigma$ with $C$ latent channels and $L$ padded sequence positions. The diagonal Gaussian posterior is sampled elementwise as
\begin{equation}
 z=\mu+\exp(\ell)\odot\xi,\qquad \xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),
\end{equation}
where $\odot$ denotes elementwise multiplication. The retrieved implementation pads sequences to 48 positions; $C$ and $L$ below denote the latent channel count and padded length, respectively. Padding positions participate in the reconstruction objective.

\subsubsection{Flow-Based Prior}

The flow maps a sampled posterior latent $z$ to a standard-normal base variable $f_\psi(z;D)$, conditioned on the aligned triplet descriptors $D$. It consists of four additive residual coupling layers interleaved with channel flips. The conditional prior density is
\begin{equation}
 p_\psi(z\mid D)=\pi\bigl(f_\psi(z;D)\bigr)\left|\det J_{f_\psi}(z;D)\right|,
\end{equation}
where $\pi$ is the standard-normal density and $J_{f_\psi}$ is the Jacobian with respect to $z$. The implemented mean-only couplings and flips preserve volume, so $\log|\det J_{f_\psi}|=0$. The flow operates on $z$, with $D$ as a condition; it does not transform $D$ into a latent sample.

For a mini-batch of $B$ sequences, let $y_{bj}$ be the integer residue code at position $j$, $r_{bj}$ the decoder's scalar output, and $N_{\mathrm{res}}$ the number of non-padding positions in the batch. The task uses
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{res}}}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{j=1}^{L}(r_{bj}-y_{bj})^2,
\end{equation}
including padding positions in the numerator. With one posterior sample $z_b$ per input, its implemented KL term is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{KL}}=\frac{1}{C}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\bigl[&\log q_\phi(z_b\mid X_b,D_b,G_b)\\
 &-\log\pi(f_\psi(z_b;D_b))\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the log densities sum over latent channels and padded positions. The training objective is $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{KL}}$, where $\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}$ is the configured weight. The encoder, decoder, and flow have parameters $\phi$, $\theta$, and $\psi$, respectively. The flow contributes through this prior term. The subsequent diffusion model uses the posterior parameters, and its final decoding path does not call the inverse flow.

\subsubsection{Amino Acid Sequence Reconstruction}

As described in the historical manuscript, the sampled latent sequence is decoded by WaveNet-style gated convolutional layers and a one-dimensional convolutional projection. In the retrieved implementation, $D_\theta(z)$ produces one real-valued residue code per position; it is trained using the squared-error objective above. It does not use the separate Transformer modules present elsewhere in the repository.

For generation, define $\mathcal{Q}$ as the implemented conversion from scalar outputs to a peptide: clamp each output to $[0,20]$, round to the nearest integer, map codes 0--19 to amino acids, and stop at the first padding code 20. Thus the generated sequence is $\mathcal{Q}(D_\theta(z))$. This specifies the implemented discretization without assuming a categorical softmax likelihood.

\subsection{Conditionally Controlled Diffusion in AMP Generative Model}

The historical manuscript describes generating sequence distribution parameters before sampling a latent sequence for decoding. In the retrieved root \texttt{diffusion.py}, the pretrained VAE is fixed and the clean diffusion input is
\begin{equation}
 U_0=\operatorname{stack}(\mu,\ell),\qquad \ell=\log\sigma,
\end{equation}
a two-channel parameter tensor with $C\times L$ entries per channel. The sampled or flow-transformed latent is not the diffusion target.

Let $\beta_s$ be the fixed linear noise schedule, $\alpha_s=1-\beta_s$, and $\bar\alpha_t=\prod_{s=0}^{t}\alpha_s$. Here $T$ denotes the last nonzero index of the retrieved schedule: its arrays are indexed from 0 through $T$, its cumulative product includes index 0, and training samples $t$ from $\{1,\ldots,T\}$. The noisy input is
\begin{equation}
 U_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}U_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,\qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I).
\end{equation}
A U-Net noise predictor $\epsilon_\omega$ is trained with mean squared error over all tensor entries. A single discrete class embedding is added to the time embedding and injected into the downsampling and upsampling blocks. Labels are dropped with probability 0.1 using one decision for the mini-batch. These are source implementation details; they do not establish the biological validity of the dataset labels.

During generation, the reverse-update mean is
\begin{equation}
 M_t=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\left(U_t-\frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\epsilon_\omega(U_t,t,c)\right).
\end{equation}
The update uses fixed variance $\beta_t I$ for $t>1$ and no added noise at $t=1$. After denoising, split $U_0$ into $\widehat\mu$ and $\widehat\ell$, sample $z=\widehat\mu+\exp(\widehat\ell)\odot\xi$ with independent $\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, and decode using $\mathcal{Q}(D_\theta(z))$. Algorithms~\ref{alg:training} and~\ref{alg:generation} describe this root-script path with label conditioning and without the optional additional classifier-free-guidance mixing.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Parameter-space diffusion training in the retrieved implementation}
\label{alg:training}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Sequence/descriptor/label data $(X,D,G,c)$, fixed encoder $E_\phi$, schedule $\{\beta_s\}_{s=0}^{T}$
\ENSURE Noise predictor $\epsilon_\omega$
\REPEAT
\STATE Sample a mini-batch $(X,D,G,c)$
\STATE $(\mu,\ell)\leftarrow E_\phi(X,D,G)$; $U_0\leftarrow\operatorname{stack}(\mu,\ell)$
\STATE For each sample, draw $t\sim\operatorname{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,T\}$ and $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$
\STATE $U_t\leftarrow\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}U_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon$
\STATE With probability 0.1, set all conditioning labels in the mini-batch to $\varnothing$
\STATE $\widehat\epsilon\leftarrow\epsilon_\omega(U_t,t,c)$
\STATE Update $\omega$ using $\operatorname{mean}((\epsilon-\widehat\epsilon)^2)$
\UNTIL{the configured training schedule ends}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Parameter generation followed by latent sampling and decoding}
\label{alg:generation}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Fixed noise predictor $\epsilon_\omega$, decoder $D_\theta$, label $c$, schedule $\{\beta_s\}_{s=0}^{T}$
\ENSURE Generated peptide sequence $X_{\mathrm{new}}$
\STATE Initialize $U_T\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ with two $C\times L$ channels
\FOR{$t=T$ down to $1$}
\STATE $\widehat\epsilon\leftarrow\epsilon_\omega(U_t,t,c)$
\STATE $M_t\leftarrow\alpha_t^{-1/2}\left(U_t-\frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\widehat\epsilon\right)$
\IF{$t>1$}
\STATE Draw $\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$; $U_{t-1}\leftarrow M_t+\sqrt{\beta_t}\eta$
\ELSE
\STATE $U_0\leftarrow M_t$
\ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE $(\widehat\mu,\widehat\ell)\leftarrow\operatorname{split}(U_0)$
\STATE Draw $\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$; $z\leftarrow\widehat\mu+\exp(\widehat\ell)\odot\xi$
\STATE $X_{\mathrm{new}}\leftarrow\mathcal{Q}(D_\theta(z))$
\RETURN $X_{\mathrm{new}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Discussion}
The two-stage design combines sequence-derived physicochemical descriptors with conditional parameter-space diffusion. The historical manuscripts, code archive, supplier documents, and MIC tables now provide a traceable basis for correcting the implementation description. They do not yet establish a reproducible architectural gain or label-specific antibacterial control.

\subsection{Relation to existing generative designs}
AMPGen combines MSA-conditioned sequence diffusion with successive physicochemical, discriminator and target-specific scoring stages~\cite{jin2025ampgen}. PRO-LDM learns an autoencoder with a parallel fitness regressor and uses fitness-conditioned diffusion in the encoded latent space~\cite{zhang2025proldm}. Table~\ref{tab:mechanism_positioning} locates the inspected implementation relative to these designs. Its diffusion target comprises posterior parameters, followed by latent sampling and decoding. The effect of that choice on generation quality or biological control remains untested.

Other relevant precedents include PepVAE's reference-based latent-space sampling and separate MIC regressors~\cite{li2021pepvae}, HydrAMP's conditional VAE and additional ranking and molecular-dynamics preselection~\cite{szymczak2023discovering}, and VAE-based latent diffusion for AMP generation~\cite{jin2024artificial}. Published validation outcomes use their respective data, selection procedures and assays; they do not establish a performance ordering against the retrospective records here.

A test of the present design must isolate the diffusion target and descriptor inputs while controlling data membership, model capacity, training budget, candidate selection and assay endpoints. Resource provenance also remains unresolved: this revision has not established whether code, training data, candidate peptides, synthesis or MIC batches, or figures are shared with AMPGen or other published or submitted studies. The required links are file versions, sequence identifiers, batch identifiers and dates; architectural differences alone do not establish independent experimental evidence.

The remaining scientific dependencies are concrete: historical run/configuration and checkpoint linkage; complete source data and label provenance; exact and similarity-based overlap audits; a full generated-pool and selection ledger; same-data, same-budget baselines and capacity-controlled ablations; and sample-linked assay rules, replicate identities, and raw readings. Target-label versus observed-spectrum analysis must preserve failures and undefined outcomes. Predicted structures and calculated descriptors must remain separate from measured stability, toxicity, or safety. The supplied numerical records can support retrospective descriptive analyses under explicit rules; they cannot resolve missing experimental provenance by themselves.

\section{Conclusion}
This revision reconciles the author's two-stage AMP design with the retrieved conditional convolutional VAE and posterior-parameter diffusion implementation. Its accompanying provenance archive preserves the historical exhibits, and the manuscript retains negative and undefined outcomes while separating them from transparent retrospective MIC counts. The available evidence does not yet support a validated success rate, an independent generalization claim, or superiority over matched baselines. These conclusions require the outstanding run and assay evidence and an independent review of the revised manuscript.
